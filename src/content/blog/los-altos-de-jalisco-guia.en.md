This Los Altos de Jalisco travel guide is for anyone whose retreat is near Guadalajara, in a region they had never heard of. The short version: Los Altos is a high plateau of red earth, blue agave and open sky, just over an hour east of the city. Spend a day in Tepatitlán for the market and its churches, eat carnitas and spiced highland birria, and come between November and April, when it barely rains.

We write from the inside. The Finca sits outside Tepatitlán, right in this landscape.

## Where Los Altos is, and why it has that name

Los Altos means "the highlands". A [2005 study from the University of Guadalajara](https://espiral.cucsh.udg.mx/index.php/EEES/article/view/1582) puts it plainly: people here are *alteños* because they live between 1,800 and 2,400 meters above sea level. The same paper describes plains that recall the Castilian plateau in Spain, where many of the first settlers came from in the 16th century.

Tepatitlán de Morelos belongs to the Altos Sur region. According to the [2024 municipal profile from Jalisco's statistics institute (IIEG)](https://iieg.gob.mx/ns/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Tepatitl%C3%A1n-de-Morelos.pdf), the town center sits at 1,806 meters and the municipality had 150,190 residents in the 2020 census. The University of Guadalajara researchers call it the southern anchor of highland identity.

The history runs deep. The IIEG notes that the Tecuexe people lived here before the Spanish arrived in 1530. Franciscan friars built a church to Saint Francis of Assisi, and the town took the name San Francisco de Tecpatitlán.

## The landscape: red earth, agave and big skies

The first thing you notice is the color of the ground. A 2005 travel piece in [México Desconocido](https://www.mexicodesconocido.com.mx/por-los-altos-de-jalisco-montanas-azules-y-campanas-al-alba.html) describes the "fiery red" soil and the blue patches on the hills that turn out, up close, to be agave fields.

That agave is the tequila plant. In 2025 the state of Jalisco launched the [Los Altos Tequila Route](https://mexiconewsdaily.com/culture/jaliscos-los-altos-tequila-route-opens-new-path-for-rural-tourism-ahead-of-2026-world-cup/), linking eight highland towns: Acatic, Tepatitlán, San Ignacio Cerro Gordo, Arandas, Jesús María, Atotonilco El Alto, Ayotlán and Tototlán. Mexico News Daily describes the "red-clay hills east of Guadalajara" and notes that the iron-rich soil and cooler climate are said to give sweeter agave.

At the Finca you live in that landscape without going anywhere. The Agaves rooms look out over the agave fields, and a hike up Cerro de la Campana shows you the whole valley from above. You will find it with other outings under [Experiences](/en/experiences).

## A day in Tepatitlán: the town nearest the Finca

Locals call it "Tepa". The official tourism guide [Visit Mexico](https://visitmexico.com/en/itinerario/51/un-dia-en-tepatitlan-de-morelos) calls it "the Pearl of Los Altos de Jalisco" and suggests this route:

1. **Morning at Mercado Centenario.** Eat at the food stalls on the first floor: menudo, tacos, gorditas, tortas.
2. **A chocomil.** A frozen treat served in a plastic bag, in cajeta, vanilla, strawberry or chocolate.
3. **The Municipal Museum**, steps from the market, in a neoclassical mansion from 1904.
4. **The Sanctuary of the Lord of Mercy.** The guide recommends a guided tour to learn about its murals.

Add the neoclassical Parish of San Francisco de Asís and its quiet plaza. México Desconocido calls Tepatitlán one of the most harmonious towns in the highlands. Local paper [El Informador](https://www.informador.mx/jalisco/tepatitlan-el-municipio-de-jalisco-donde-descansar-es-la-ley-y-comer-delicioso-es-la-regla-20260419-0001.html) also mentions evening legend tours.

### With more than one day

- **Arandas**, on the same tequila route. Back in 2005 it already had 16 distilleries, according to México Desconocido.
- **The Cristero trail.** The Cristero War (1926 to 1929) reached its greatest intensity in Los Altos, as [México Desconocido](https://www.mexicodesconocido.com.mx/altos-de-jalisco-memoria-de-la-cristiada.html) explains. The National Cristero Museum is in Encarnación de Díaz.
- **San Miguel el Alto**, built in pink stone.

## Highland food: what to order

Los Altos has been cattle country since colonial times, and you taste it. For Tepatitlán, [El Informador](https://www.informador.mx/jalisco/no-solo-guadalajara-los-municipios-de-jalisco-con-los-sabores-mas-autenticos-20260814-0112.html) recommends:

- **Highland-style carnitas**, "the local star".
- **Birria alteña**, a stew with a more spiced seasoning.
- **Tacos de carnaza** (beef) and **chicharrón prensado** (pressed pork crackling).

Around the region, look for local cheese, cajeta (a soft milk caramel) and pan con nata, a cream bread.

At the Finca, the food is made with what grows in the greenhouse. If you want to cook, there is a kitchen and bread workshop with handmade tortillas and a wood-fired oven.

## When to go: the weather by season

The highlands have two clear seasons, a dry one and a rainy one. The [IIEG profile](https://iieg.gob.mx/ns/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Tepatitl%C3%A1n-de-Morelos.pdf) gives Tepatitlán an average temperature of 17.8 °C (64 °F), with average lows of 5.4 °C (42 °F) and average highs of 30.2 °C (86 °F).

The [climate risk report from Tepatitlán's planning institute (IMPLAN)](https://tepatitlan.gob.mx/IMPLAN/documentos/Tepatitl%C3%A1n_ARVC_Versi%C3%B3n_Final.pdf) shows rainfall month by month. From November to April only about 52 mm falls, roughly 6 % of the year's rain.

| Season | Average rain | What to expect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| November to February | 6 to 17 mm a month | Dry. Cold mornings and nights: pack a warm layer |
| March to May | 3 to 20 mm a month | March and April are the driest months |
| June to September | 134 to 249 mm a month | The rainy season, peaking in July. The fields turn green |
| October | About 53 mm | The rains taper off |

The same report counts 280 sunny days and about 18 days of frost a year. If you come in winter, plan for chilly evenings and early mornings.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where is Los Altos de Jalisco?**
East and northeast of Guadalajara, on a plateau between 1,800 and 2,400 meters high. Tepatitlán, in the Altos Sur region, is just over an hour from the city.

**What is the best time of year to visit Los Altos?**
November to April, the dry season. Winter nights are cold, so bring a warm layer. From June to September the rains arrive and the fields turn green.

**What should I eat in Tepatitlán?**
Highland-style carnitas, birria alteña, tacos de carnaza and chicharrón prensado. At Mercado Centenario, try a chocomil.

**Is there transport from Guadalajara airport to the Finca?**
Yes. We offer round-trip transport from the airport or anywhere in the city, at an extra cost. Ask for it when you book or when your organizer requests a quote.

**Is Los Altos worth it if I have never heard of it?**
It is a region of ranches, agave and working towns, far from the pace of the beach resorts. If you want quiet and home cooking, it is a good place to start.
