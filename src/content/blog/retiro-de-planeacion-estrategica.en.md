A good strategic planning retreat agenda rests on three things: one guiding question, data prepared before anyone arrives, and a close with owners and dates. Two days with one night in between is enough to review the year, choose a few priorities and leave with real commitments.

Taking the team away from the office helps for a practical reason: nobody drifts back to their desk between sessions. Below is an hour-by-hour plan you can adapt for a leadership team getting ready for 2027.

## Why plan away from the office

At the office, a planning session competes with email, fires to put out and the four o'clock meeting. Away from it, the team shares meals, walks and downtime, and the conversation keeps going after the session ends.

Planning is also not budgeting. In a [2026 column for Mexico's El Financiero](https://www.elfinanciero.com.mx/opinion/carlos-ruiz-gonzalez/2026/06/19/el-viaje-es-la-recompensa-por-que-importa-mas-planear-que-tener-un-plan/), Carlos Ruiz González puts it plainly: a budget assigns numbers to what you already do, while planning asks whether you should be doing something different. People, he adds, defend the plans they helped build.

There is a human argument too. Google's research on [the five dynamics of effective teams](https://business.google.com/us/think/future-of-marketing/five-dynamics-effective-team/), covering more than 200 teams, found that the most important one is psychological safety: feeling secure enough to take risks, admit mistakes and offer ideas. A day and a half together, with shared meals and a night in between, gives a conversation time to get there.

## Before the retreat: data, people and one guiding question

Most of the work behind a good retreat happens two or three weeks ahead. Use this as a starting checklist:

1. **One guiding question.** For example: "What three things must go right in 2027 for the year to count?" Every block of the agenda serves that question.
2. **All the data in one folder.** Results against targets, key indicators, customer and employee surveys. Send it a week ahead so nobody reads it for the first time in the room.
3. **A SWOT analysis done in advance.** Writing in [Forbes México](https://www.forbes.com.mx/red-forbes-empresa-sin-planeacion-estrategica-es-como-un-barco-a-la-deriva/), Mario Rizo Rivas suggests starting with strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats, then moving to measurable objectives and the indicators that track them. Ask each leader for a written SWOT before the trip.
4. **Who attends and who facilitates.** Invite the people who will carry out the plan. A CEO who also runs the session struggles to listen; an outside consultant or an experienced HR lead can facilitate instead.
5. **What gets decided.** Agree beforehand which topics leave with a decision and which only with a proposal.

### Why a walk in the middle of the day

The walk is part of the work, not a reward. A [2014 Stanford study](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0036577) ran four experiments and found that walking boosted the generation of new ideas during the walk and for a while afterwards, with outdoor walks producing the most novel analogies. A [2012 study in PLOS ONE](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0051474) of 56 hikers measured about 50% better scores on a creativity test after four days in nature without devices. The sample is small, and the authors could not separate the effect of nature from the effect of leaving phones behind.

Breaks matter as well. In [a 2021 Microsoft study](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/brain-research) of 14 people wearing EEG caps, stress built up over four back-to-back meetings and held steady when there were 10-minute breaks between them. It is preliminary evidence, but it matches what any team feels by six in the evening.

## The evening: dinner and a bonfire for a different kind of talk

After dinner, a bonfire changes the tone. With no screen and no flip chart, people tell stories instead of reporting progress.

Anthropologist Polly Wiessner documented that shift in a [2014 PNAS study](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1404212111) of the Ju/'hoan people of southern Africa: daytime talk centred on economic matters and complaints about others, while talk around the fire at night turned to stories. It is research on another culture, so take it as inspiration rather than a formula.

One simple prompt for the fire: each person shares a moment this year when the team worked well and one when it did not. No debate. Whatever comes up gets written down the next morning, not that night.

## Day 2: priorities, owners and dates

Day two looks ahead and ends with decisions on paper.

| Time | Block |
| --- | --- |
| 7:30 am | Optional walk or yoga |
| 8:00 am | Breakfast |
| 9:00 am | Ten minutes to note what came up last night |
| 9:15 am | Strategic options: from a long list to three to five priorities |
| 11:00 am | Break and coffee; room check-out by 12:00 pm |
| 11:30 am | For each priority: an owner, an indicator and a first review date |
| 1:00 pm | Commitment round: each leader names what they will stop doing to deliver |
| 1:30 pm | Lunch and drive back to Guadalajara |

### Ask for commitment out loud

In *The Five Dysfunctions of a Team* ([Jossey-Bass, 2002](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/The+Five+Dysfunctions+of+a+Team:+A+Leadership+Fable,+20th+Anniversary+Edition-p-9780787960759)), Patrick Lencioni describes a common problem on leadership teams: people who appear to back a decision they never truly accepted. His answer runs through open conflict before the decision and clear commitment after it.

That is why the final round is not a formality. If someone cannot say what they will stop doing, the priority is not theirs yet.

Harvard professor Amy Edmondson, who studies psychological safety, explains what it is and why it matters at work in her [2014 TEDx talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LhoLuui9gX8). It is short, and worth having the team watch before the retreat.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should a strategic planning retreat be?**
For a leadership team, two days with one night usually works if the diagnosis is prepared beforehand. A single day leaves little room between reviewing the year and deciding. More than two days makes sense when the business model itself is on the table.

**Who should facilitate the planning session?**
Ideally someone with no position to defend: an outside consultant or an experienced HR lead. That way the CEO can take part and listen instead of moderating.

**How many priorities should come out of the retreat?**
Few. Three to five priorities for the year, each with an owner, an indicator and a first review date. A list of fifteen is no longer a list of priorities.

**What happens after the retreat?**
Book the first review before you leave the venue, ideally 30 days out. Share a one-page summary of the priorities and their owners with the rest of the company.
