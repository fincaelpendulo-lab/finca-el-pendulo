The outdoor team building activities that work best have a clear purpose, are optional and leave time to talk. A walk in pairs, a hands-on workshop, a long lunch and an evening by the fire usually do more for a team than a scavenger hunt with prizes.

If you have been asked to plan an offsite, this guide helps you choose by goal (trust, communication, leadership or rest) and build a day or a weekend without anyone feeling forced to have fun.

## What team building is for (and why so much of it misses)

Team building does work. A [2009 meta-analysis in Small Group Research](https://doi.org/10.1177/1046496408328821) pooled 60 correlations and found a moderate positive effect. It was strongest for how a team feels and how it coordinates.

The same paper describes four components: setting goals, building relationships, solving problems together and clarifying roles. Use them as a menu. Decide which of the four your team needs before you pick any activity.

Most offsites miss because of how they are run, not what is on the program. In a field experiment, Wharton professors [Ethan Mollick and Nancy Rothbard](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/gamification-powering-game/) found that a workplace game lifted the mood of people who bought into it and lowered the mood of those who did not. Giving people a choice increased buy-in.

The practical lesson: offer options, explain what each block is for, and let people watch rather than join if they prefer.

## Outdoor team building activities for trust

Google studied 180 teams in Project Aristotle. According to [its re:Work guide](https://rework.withgoogle.com/intl/en/guides/understand-team-effectiveness), the most important dynamic was psychological safety, and who was on a team mattered less than how the team worked together.

The term comes from [Amy Edmondson's 1999 study](https://doi.org/10.2307/2666999) of 51 teams at a manufacturing company: a shared belief that the team is safe for interpersonal risk, such as asking a question or admitting a mistake. In [The Fearless Organization](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/The+Fearless+Organization%3A+Creating+Psychological+Safety+in+the+Workplace+for+Learning%2C+Innovation%2C+and+Growth-p-9781119477242) (Wiley, 2018) she puts it in practical terms: people need to voice half-finished ideas, ask questions and admit errors without fear. In [her 2017 TED talk](https://www.ted.com/talks/amy_edmondson_how_to_turn_a_group_of_strangers_into_a_team) she describes what helps a group of strangers respond as a team to a new problem.

Trust grows from small, repeated experiences, not from a speech. Activities that help:

- **A hike in pairs.** At the Finca, the [Cerro de la Campana hike](/en/experiences) climbs to a view of the valley and the agave fields. Before you set out, give each pair one simple question: "What part of your job does almost nobody see?"
- **Caring for the horses.** Brushing and feeding a horse in the stables takes calm and attention. Nobody is an expert, so job titles fade.
- **A round of lessons learned.** Each person shares one mistake from the year and what it taught them. When the leader goes first, others follow.

## Activities for communication and teamwork

Here you want tasks with a visible result, where people have to agree without a manual. Busy hands lower the tension and conversation comes on its own.

- **Pottery.** The Finca art studio has five pottery wheels. It works well in small groups that take turns and teach each other.
- **Cooking and bread.** The cooking workshop uses produce from the garden and a wood-fired talavera oven. Splitting tasks, watching the clock and serving everyone else is teamwork in its plainest form.
- **Garden and greenhouse.** Planting or harvesting together is calm and suits people who would rather not be in the spotlight.
- **A challenge with a deliverable.** A mural, a table set for everyone, or a three-minute pitch on "the team we want to be next year".

If your team needs more structured work, the Finca offers [leadership activities and workshops](/en/experiences) built around communication, trust and working together.

## Leadership with horses: the horse as a mirror

A horse responds to what you do, not to your title. If your intention and your body say different things, it will not follow you. That is why horses show up in executive development programs.

A [2025 review in Administrative Sciences](https://doi.org/10.3390/admsci15080298) links equine-assisted learning with self-awareness, nonverbal communication and trust building. The exercises happen on the ground: leading the horse, asking it to walk on or stop. It is a narrative review, not a meta-analysis, so treat it as early evidence.

The Finca stables have nine horses; five were born here. If you would like a leadership session with horses for your team, ask us when you request a quote. For something simpler, there are also horse rides around the Finca, among trees and agave.

## Breaks that bring people together: shared meals and the fire

Breaks are not lost time. A Cornell study across more than 50 firehouses found that crews who [ate together more often](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2015/11/groups-eat-together-perform-better-together) got higher performance ratings from their supervisors. It is a correlation, not proof of cause, but it matches what most people have seen at work.

At the Finca, El 27 is a greenhouse dining room with a long table among plants, and Sammasati has a table for 25 beside the fire pit. Seat people mixed, not by department.

The fire has its own logic. Anthropologist Polly Wiessner of the University of Utah [reported in PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1404212111) that in one southern African community, daytime talk dealt with practical matters, while talk around the evening fire turned to stories that helped people understand one another and ease the day's tensions. A fire with no agenda and one closing question works well.

## How to plan your outdoor team building day or weekend

Set the goal first, then pick one or two activities per block. This table sums up the options:

| Goal | Activity | Time of day | Group |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Trust | Hike in pairs to Cerro de la Campana | Morning | Pairs |
| Trust | Caring for the horses | Morning or afternoon | Small groups |
| Communication | Pottery or art workshop | Afternoon | In turns |
| Communication | Cooking and wood-fired bread | Midday | Task teams |
| Leadership | Session with horses | Morning | Small groups |
| Rest | Long lunch and evening fire | Lunch and night | Whole team |

**One day (no overnight):** arrival and coffee, hike in pairs, a working session in the [Ágora Gnosis room](/en/spaces), a long lunch, an afternoon workshop and a 30-minute closing round.

**A weekend (with lodging):** day one starts after check-in (2:00 pm) with a short session, dinner and the fire. Day two brings the hike or the horses in the morning, work in the meeting room, lunch and a workshop. Day three closes with agreements before check-out (12:00 pm).

For groups of 12 or more, the [retreat package](/en/retreats) includes lodging, three meals a day, coffee breaks and use of the spaces. Experiences are added separately.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which team building activities work without feeling forced?**
The ones with a stated purpose that let people choose. Offer two options per block and let people watch instead of join. Walking, cooking or a hands-on workshop usually feel less forced than competitions.

**How long should a team offsite last?**
One day is enough to get to know each other better. If you want deeper conversations, a night away helps: dinner and the evening fire do a lot of the work.

**How many people do I need for a team retreat at the Finca?**
The retreat package is for groups of 12 or more. For smaller groups, write to us and we will look at lodging or Day Pass options.

**Are the horse activities safe for people who have never ridden?**
Caring for the horses and ground exercises do not involve riding. Anyone who would rather keep their distance can watch. The horse ride is optional.
