Here is **how to price a retreat** so it pays even when it is not full: add up what each guest costs you (the venue's lodging and meals, materials), then spread your fixed costs (your own pay, co-facilitators, marketing) over the smallest group you are willing to run with. Add the two and you have your price per person. Price it for a full house and every empty bed comes out of your pocket.

This guide is the spreadsheet in plain words, with a worked example in Mexican pesos (MXN). The figures are illustrative. They are not the Finca's rates or any other venue's.

## Before you price a retreat: what kind, and for whom

Pricing starts before the maths. A yoga weekend for your regular students is a different product from a week for travellers flying in from abroad. Pin down three things:

- **Length**: how many nights and how many meals.
- **Group**: how many places you open, and the minimum at which the retreat goes ahead.
- **What is included**: sessions, materials, extra activities, transfers.

Look at what similar retreats in the area charge, too. Jen Corley's [pricing guide for WeTravel Academy](https://academy.wetravel.com/price-yoga-retreat) treats that as a rough guide only: every retreat is different, and someone else's price tells you nothing about whether yours covers your costs.

## Per-person costs: lodging, meals and what the venue includes

Per-person (variable) costs rise with every guest who signs up. The venue is usually the biggest one.

Many venues quote a package per person. At the Finca, for example, the retreat package covers **lodging, three meals a day, a coffee break and use of the spaces**. Always ask what is in and what costs extra; we go through the questions in [how to choose a retreat venue](/en/blog/choosing-a-retreat-venue).

Then add:

- Materials or a welcome kit (journals, oils, a small gift to take home).
- Any extra activity you pay for per guest.
- Card processing fees, if you take cards.

In our example, all of that comes to **MXN 3,800 per person** for a weekend.

## Fixed costs: your pay, co-facilitators and marketing

Fixed costs stay the same whether 8 or 20 people come. This is where first-time leaders go wrong most often, because they forget to pay themselves.

Retreat strategist Paloma Neuman makes the point on [her 2017 blog post](https://rockyouryogaretreat.squarespace.com/blog/retreat-pricing): a retreat leader is "on" the whole time, not only in class, and many teachers underprice. WeTravel adds the hidden costs: the classes you will not teach that week, childcare, someone to look after your pets.

A starter list:

- **Your pay**: hours of planning, marketing and the retreat days themselves. Put a figure on it, as if you were hiring someone else.
- **Co-facilitators** and support (photography, music, an assistant).
- **Marketing**: ads, design, photos.
- **Your own travel** and personal costs.
- **A buffer** for surprises. Both Neuman and [Bookinglayer's guide to organizing yoga retreats](https://bookinglayer.com/article/a-guide-for-organising-yoga-retreats) say to leave room for what you did not plan.

In the example: your pay MXN 15,000, a co-facilitator MXN 6,000, marketing MXN 3,000, your travel MXN 1,000 and a MXN 2,500 buffer. Total: **MXN 27,500 in fixed costs**.

## Break-even: how many guests you need not to lose money

Break-even is the group size at which you cover every cost and make neither profit nor loss. The open textbook [Principles of Accounting, Volume 2 (OpenStax, 2019)](https://openstax.org/books/principles-managerial-accounting/pages/3-2-calculate-a-break-even-point-in-units-and-dollars) works it out like this:

> Break-even = fixed costs ÷ (price per person minus cost per person)

The part in brackets is what each booking contributes towards your fixed costs. Charge MXN 6,800 when each guest costs you MXN 3,800 and each one contributes MXN 3,000. With MXN 27,500 in fixed costs you need 9.2 guests, which means **10**.

Here is the same price with different group sizes:

| Guests | Revenue (MXN) | Total costs (MXN) | Result (MXN) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | 54,400 | 57,900 | lose 3,500 |
| 10 | 68,000 | 65,500 | make 2,500 |
| 12 | 81,600 | 73,100 | make 8,500 |
| 15 | 102,000 | 84,500 | make 17,500 |
| 20 | 136,000 | 103,500 | make 32,500 |

Your own pay is already inside the costs, so whatever is left is profit for the business on top of your fee.

### Work backwards from your minimum

The safest way to price a retreat is to choose the minimum first. Neuman's example opens 20 places and prices for 10 to 12; WeTravel's opens 15 and covers everything with 8 to 12. The formula:

> Price = cost per person + (fixed costs ÷ minimum group size)

With a minimum of 10: MXN 3,800 + MXN 2,750 = MXN 6,550. Round up, never down.

If you hold your retreat at the Finca, there is one more number to plan around: the minimum group is **12 people, including your staff**. The package covers lodging, meals and use of the facilities for all 12, you and your team included.

That changes the sums in two ways. If you bring one co-facilitator, your minimum is 10 paying guests. And your team's packages become a fixed cost, because you pay for them however few people sign up.

Using the same illustrative figures, and assuming a staff package costs the same as a guest's:

- Fixed costs: MXN 27,500 + 2 packages at MXN 3,800 = MXN 35,100.
- Price: MXN 3,800 + (MXN 35,100 ÷ 10) = MXN 7,310. Rounded up, **MXN 7,400**.

Every staff member is one fewer paying place and one more package in your fixed costs. Run the numbers with your actual team.

## Margin, early bird and launch pricing

Once break-even is covered, decide what you want to earn. A [2021 guide in Expansión](https://expansion.mx/carrera/2021/04/29/guia-freelancers-cuanto-tienes-que-cobrar) for freelancers in Mexico puts the average profit at 20 to 30 percent per project. That comes from other trades, not retreats, but it keeps you from settling for zero.

Price also sends a message. Pricing consultant Casey Brown put it this way in her talk [Know your worth, and then ask for it (TEDxColumbusWomen, 2015)](https://www.ted.com/talks/casey_brown_know_your_worth_and_then_ask_for_it): people pay you what they think you are worth. Hermann Simon reaches a similar point in [Confessions of the Pricing Man (Springer, 2015)](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-20400-0): of the three profit levers (price, volume and cost), price gets the least attention and has the greatest impact.

For pre-sales:

- **An early bird price** with a deadline. [Bookinglayer (2026)](https://www.bookinglayer.com/article/sell-out-yoga-retreats) notes that early pricing creates urgency.
- **Your community first**: your students and your mailing list or group chat, before any public launch.
- Work out the early bird price with the same method. It has to clear break-even too.

## How to take payment: deposits, instalments and cancellation

How you charge matters as much as how much. Two classic behavioural economics papers help:

- Drazen Prelec and George Loewenstein, writing in [Marketing Science in 1998](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.17.1.4), propose that something already paid for can be enjoyed "as if it were free". Collecting payment before the retreat lets guests live it without thinking about money.
- John Gourville and Dilip Soman, in the [Journal of Consumer Research (1998)](https://doi.org/10.1086/209533), found in experiments that the pull of a past payment on whether people use what they paid for fades over time. A distant payment weighs less than a recent one.

That is why many leaders pair a **deposit to hold the place** with **instalments** up to a few weeks before. Bookinglayer notes that payment plans lower the upfront commitment.

Put your cancellation policy in writing and line it up with your venue's: if the venue keeps part of the payment when you cancel, you need similar terms with your guests. Mexico's consumer agency, Profeco, in 2026 guidance [reported by UnoTV](https://www.unotv.com/nacional/te-vas-de-vacaciones-profeco-recuerda-derechos-al-contratar-hoteles-viajes-y-transporte/), asks that the total price, extra charges and cancellation policy be stated before payment, and that any contract spell out both sides' rights. If you will issue invoices, ask an accountant about tax.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much should I charge for a weekend retreat?**
It depends on the venue, your own pay and your minimum group. Add the venue's cost per person and your materials, divide your fixed costs by your minimum group size and add the two. Compare the result with similar retreats, as a reference only.

**What happens if my retreat does not fill?**
If you priced for your minimum rather than a full house, you cover your costs even with empty places. Set a go or no-go date and tell guests about it from the start.

**What is the minimum group size for a retreat?**
It depends on the venue and on your numbers. At the Finca the minimum is 12 people including your staff, with lodging, meals and use of the facilities in the package. For your price, count only the people who pay: with you and one co-facilitator, that is 10 guests.

**Should I take a deposit?**
Yes. A deposit holds the place and lets you pay your own deposit to the venue. Put in writing how much it is, when the other payments are due and what happens if someone cancels.

**How do I know what the venue package includes?**
Ask for it in writing: lodging, how many meals, coffee break, which spaces and for how many hours. At the Finca the package includes lodging, three meals, a coffee break and use of the spaces, and we list it all in the quote.
